# Why I chose this question

An independent work sample for a cross-functional BI and metrics analyst role. I have no access to Fortra internal data, dashboards, policy definitions or reporting problems. The synthetic case is not a claim that Fortra's dashboards disagree.

## Public reasoning

**1. Let the business question lead.** Fortra's own [Key Performance Indicators and Reporting Tools for Success](https://www.fortra.com/resources/guides/key-performance-indicators-and-reporting-tools-success) discusses aligning IT and business needs, KPIs and reporting tools. My interpretation: a measurement is useful when the person receiving it knows which decision it supports. This is my interpretation, not a quote or statement of Fortra's internal BI maturity.

**2. Explain technical performance in terms leaders can act on.** In [3 Ways Peer Insight Illustrates Cybersecurity Performance](https://www.fortra.com/blog/3-ways-peer-insight-peer-comparison-illustrates-cybersecurity-performance), Fortra describes nontechnical leadership reporting as a challenge and connects reporting to prioritization and scarce resources. My interpretation: a short exception-led decision brief is worth testing as an alternative to adding dashboards.

**3. Use definitions to improve prioritization.** The [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20) is intended to help organizations understand, assess, prioritize and communicate cybersecurity outcomes. My interpretation: metrics should carry clear scope, ownership and decision context. This demonstration does not implement or certify adherence to the NIST CSF.

## The practical offer

A short, reviewable handoff for operational leaders: (a) clarify the metric, (b) show which segment deserves a closer look, (c) identify the owner and the question to validate, and (d) record the decision for the next review. A real pilot should first ask relevant stakeholders what they already measure and how decisions are currently made.

## Important limitation

The example critical on-time completion rate is a synthetic closed-cohort indicator (critical tickets resolved within a fabricated time target divided by all critical tickets created). It counts open items as incomplete and **must not** be described as contractual SLA compliance. There is no inference about real Fortra service levels, cyber risk exposure, or operational value generated.
