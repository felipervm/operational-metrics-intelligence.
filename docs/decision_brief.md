# Decision brief — What should leadership look at first?

**Independent fictional scenario; not Fortra data or a diagnosis of its operations.**

## Decision

Before interpreting a green high-level closure KPI as evidence of operational health, investigate severity-specific SLA performance and agree on the meaning of the metrics used in Risk, Security and IT reviews.

## What the synthetic records show

- 180 fictional IT, Security and Compliance tickets; 149 resolved (82.8%), 31 open.
- 51 critical tickets; 21 resolved within the exercise’s invented time target (41.2% across the full created cohort). This is not contractual SLA compliance.
- The values are **not inconsistent calculations**: the completion and SLA indicators answer different business questions.

## Recommended conversation with functional owners

**P1 — Investigate critical-item routing:** Ask Security Operations and IT Service Management to review exception handoffs, prioritization and the illustrative ticket clock. Validate the assumed threshold against real service agreements. A low modeled rate is not evidence of a real company issue.

**P1 — Approve a metric contract:** Define the numerator, denominator, period, exclusions, accountable team and use cases. Make metric definitions available alongside dashboards and executive summaries. Don't average unlike rates.

**P2 — Pilot exception-led reviews:** Publish a compact scorecard with named owners and rationale for exceptions; compare whether the review changes the decisions leaders make rather than just dashboard engagement.

## What I would validate before deploying

Confirm dataset permissions, join keys, status mappings, SLA starts/stops and pauses, reporting intervals, reopened tickets, thresholds, severities, data-owner signoff and stakeholder decision needs. Then benchmark reviewer time, false alarms and actionability with real approved data before making claims about effectiveness or savings.
