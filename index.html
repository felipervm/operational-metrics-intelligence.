What should leadership look at first?

I kept coming back to one question in the role description: if Risk, Security and IT each report their own performance, how does leadership know what actually needs attention? I built a small, fictional example to explore a more useful answer.

04 / Decisions, not charts

What I'd put in front of the team.

I wanted the output to be useful in a meeting: one finding worth discussing, one suggested owner, and the specific questions I would check before anyone changes a process.

05 / What this offers

A small reporting contract before a big reporting system.

The thing I would offer first is a repeatable way to connect a KPI to a decision, a definition, and an accountable owner. The dashboard comes after that.

Shared KPI definitions

Numerator, denominator, scope, owner, reporting period, exclusions and escalation criteria.

Exception-led review

Surface the few severity or process indicators that merit deeper investigation, rather than multiplying dashboards.

Reproducible evidence

Transparent Python transformations, synthetic source records and automated checks for calculation consistency.

06 / Why I took this angle

Not a guess about Fortra. A question supported by its own writing.

While reading about the role, I looked for material that would help me understand why reporting governance matters. I found three useful references, and they pushed me toward decisions and metric definitions rather than a bigger dashboard.

Start with the business question

Fortra's KPI guide says reporting should address business information needs and help IT and business teams align. That made me focus on what a metric is supposed to help someone decide.

Explain risk to nontechnical leaders

Fortra has written about the difficulty of communicating security performance to leaders juggling competing priorities. I took that as a reason to lead with a short review brief rather than technical charts.

Measure to prioritize

NIST CSF 2.0 describes a common language for assessing, prioritizing, and communicating cybersecurity outcomes. This case applies that principle to a fictional cross-functional operations review; it does not claim CSF compliance.

A note on scope

I would start with the decision, not the dashboard.

I have not seen Fortra’s internal reporting, and I am not proposing that its metrics are wrong. This is how I would approach the work: clarify what leaders need to decide, agree on definitions with the teams responsible, and bring a short, evidence-backed list of exceptions to the conversation. The fictional figures are a way to make that approach tangible.
